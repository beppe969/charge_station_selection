Charging-port assignment | Analyses added for the IEEE T-ITS version
====================================================================

The IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems version of the
manuscript (main_tits_v2.tex) adds three analyses to those described in
REPRODUCE_SUBMISSION.txt. Both scripts use the pinned environment in
requirements.txt (CPython 3.9, NumPy 1.20.3, SciPy 1.7.3, pandas 1.5.3,
Matplotlib 3.4.3) and the source-reconciled public inventories. From the
package root:

    python code/driver_outcomes.py --workers 2
    python code/rolling_horizon.py --workers 2

Each takes well under a minute on a laptop. Outputs go to results/tits/.

1. Per-driver outcomes of joint reassignment (driver_outcomes.py, Part 1)
   For the three cities at half load (30 layouts, three processing orders,
   seed 103721 as in the city study), every driver served by sequential cost
   booking is compared under that booking and under the minimum-cost
   reassignment of exactly the same drivers. driver_outcomes_drivers.csv.gz
   lists every driver; driver_outcomes_summary.csv gives the shares of drivers
   who gain and lose, mean gains and losses, the gain-to-loss ratio and the
   savings when no driver may lose more than 0, 2, 5, 10 or 20 dollars
   (pairs exceeding the cap are removed before solving the assignment).
   The zero-cap saving is exactly zero, as Proposition 4 of the manuscript
   (Pareto efficiency of sequential least-cost booking) predicts.
   driver_rows.tex supplies Table V.

2. DC charging taper (driver_outcomes.py, Part 2)
   Washington at half load, with the average power of a DC session falling
   linearly with the request, from the nominal effective power at 20 kWh to
   half of it at 60 kWh. With the factor set to one the code reproduces the
   published base row (4.63%). taper_summary.csv supplies the added row of
   the sensitivity table.

3. Rolling-horizon dispatch (rolling_horizon.py)
   Washington, six hours of Poisson arrivals at 1.5, 3.0 or 4.5 requests per
   minute, 30 layouts per rate (arrival seed [103721, 0, layout, 60607, rate
   index]). Reservations hold a port from confirmation until departure
   (travel at 22 km/h, site wait, charging session); requests unassigned
   after 30 minutes are lost with a 100-dollar fallback. Immediate least-cost
   booking is compared with maximum-service minimum-cost batches every 1, 2,
   5, 10, 15, 20 or 30 minutes; confirmation delay is valued at 0.224 dollars
   per minute. rolling_summary.csv holds paired differences with 95%
   bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples, seed 4577); rolling_horizon.pdf is
   Fig. 3 of the manuscript and rolling_rows.tex a table of the same results.

4. Occupancy price and robustness of the rolling-horizon comparison
   (rolling_horizon.py --extensions)

    python code/rolling_horizon.py --extensions --workers 2

   (a) A fast-port occupancy price kappa (dollars per minute of holding time)
       is added to the dispatcher's scores but not to reported costs.
       rolling_kappa_tuning.csv tunes kappa on layouts 100-109, separate from
       the evaluation layouts; kappa = 0.8 maximizes service at 4.5 requests
       per minute. rolling_throughput_summary.csv evaluates immediate booking
       and batches of 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes with kappa = 0 and 0.8 on
       layouts 0-29.
   (b) rolling_robustness_summary.csv repeats the comparison with a three-
       hour warm-up in a nine-hour horizon (requests arriving after 180
       minutes are counted), patience of 15 or 60 minutes, and fallback
       costs of 50 or 200 dollars. It supplies Table VII of the manuscript.
   Paired intervals use 2,000 bootstrap resamples of whole layouts (seed
   4577). The run takes about one minute with two workers.
